# HH-12: `fb export` proof and decommission

Goal: prove the exit promise (the `hh` app leaves the platform as a Compose bundle of the Go binary, Postgres and the static bundles, and runs anywhere from a restore), retire the HardscapeOS and HH Pro deployments and repositories to archive, and hand the Gable sequence the shared packages it assumes.
Preconditions: HH-11 (core sole writer, ledger closed, decommission list produced); backups running with a restore drill recorded (S0).
Position relative to the ERP cutover session: downstream. Nothing here can begin while a second writer exists, and nothing here is needed for any role to work.
Harness: mixed. HH-12.1 and HH-12.4 on Claude (together they prove a security and continuity property); HH-12.2 and HH-12.3 on Kimi or Kilo. Integrator and verifier on Claude.

Lanes (parallel unless marked):
- HH-12.1 The export proof
  Scope: `fb export` producing the bundle (binary with `serve`, `worker`, `migrate`; a Postgres image with the schema; the static micro-app bundles; the manifest); a restore of the anonymised seed (HH-5.3) into it on a machine that is not the platform; the readiness report and both integration suites run against the exported stack; the JWKS dependency satisfied by a documented standalone identity path so the exported app is not dead without the platform (the shape's exit promise is the bundle "runs anywhere").
  Touches: `hh/docs/runbooks/export-proof.md`, `hh/scripts/export-verify/`.
  Acceptance: the exported stack boots from the bundle and the seed with no reference to a platform host; every micro-app loads and one job per micro-app is performed against it; the readiness report is green; the bundle contains no secret (a scan) and no platform hostname.
  Prompt: "Run `fb export` for `hh`, restore the anonymised seed into the exported bundle on a machine outside the platform, run the readiness report and both integration suites, perform one job per micro-app, and scan the bundle for secrets and platform hostnames. Record the result as the export proof runbook. No em or en dashes; no dates."

- HH-12.2 Decommission
  Scope: from the HH-11 decommission list: the HardscapeOS staging and demo droplet services stopped and their unit files removed by their operator from the ERP's own apply-script conventions; the HH Pro static deployment removed by the reversible three steps its runbook names (`hh_pro_dibbits/deploy/RUNBOOK.md`); the read-only ERP process retired after the retention window; both repositories archived with a final README pointing at `hh` and the analysis; the four `PARK-*.patch` files and the parked notes recorded as history in `hh/docs/analysis/`.
  Touches: runbooks under `hh/docs/runbooks/decommission-*.md`; the archive READMEs are the only writes to the old repositories, made by their owners after HH-11 closed.
  Acceptance: no process answers on the old hosts' application ports; the old repositories are archived; the org's Uptime Kuma monitors point only at platform hosts.
  Prompt: "Write and execute the decommission runbooks for the HardscapeOS droplet services and the HH Pro static deployment, using each product's own reversible steps, and archive both repositories with a pointer README. Nothing is deleted from history. No em or en dashes; no dates."

- HH-12.3 The Gable handover
  Scope: a package-by-package note for G-1 recording which shared packages `hh` built and what each assumes (the token vocabulary, the offline-store backends, the sync statechart's policies, the auth package's unlock flow, the generated-client convention), plus the capability flags `hh` exposes in its manifest that the implementation model (context section 5) needs, so `gable` can consume rather than rebuild.
  Touches: `hh/docs/handover/gable.md`; a proposed `capabilities:` block for `gable/manifest.yaml`.
  Acceptance: every package in `web/packages` and `statecharts` has a consuming note with its public interface and its tests named; the capability flag list maps to the ERP modules Gable's LBM vertical shares.
  Prompt: "Write the Gable handover: for each shared package and statechart, its public interface, its assumptions and the tests that pin it; and the capability flags `hh` exposes. This is what lets G-1 start with the packages present. No em or en dashes; no dates."

- HH-12.4 `impl-hh-demo` in the export proof, and the walkthroughs on the exported stack
  Scope: the export promise is that the app runs anywhere, and an implementation is how the app reaches anyone, so the proof covers both: the bundle from 12.1 is brought up on the machine outside the platform from `impl-hh-demo`'s manifest and the demo seed with its tour slices, rather than from a hand-written configuration, and the three walkthroughs are then run against it. That makes the tours the evidence for 12.1's "one job per micro-app" clause instead of a person clicking through and attesting to it. The demo org itself is not decommissioned by this session: `hh-demo` outlives the cutover as the public surface and as the source of every release note's clip, so HH-11.4's decommission list names it as retained with that reason and this lane checks that it does.
  Touches: `hh/docs/runbooks/export-proof.md`, `hh/scripts/export-verify/`, the `impl-hh-demo` repository (the `PRODUCT_TAG` the proof pins).
  Acceptance: the exported stack boots from `impl-hh-demo`'s manifest and the demo seed with no reference to a platform host; the three walkthroughs run green against it from their definitions, and their guards are the pass condition for one job per micro-app; the stubbed integration endpoints are still stubs in the export, so the exported demo cannot reach a live third party; the secret and hostname scans from 12.1 pass over the implementation as well as the product bundle; the decommission list names `hh-demo` as retained with its reason.
  Prompt: "Extend the export proof to the implementation: bring the exported bundle up on the machine outside the platform from `impl-hh-demo`'s manifest and the demo seed, then run the three walkthroughs against it from their definitions as the one-job-per-micro-app evidence. Scan the implementation for secrets and platform hostnames as well as the product bundle, and prove the stubbed integrations are still stubs. Confirm the decommission list keeps `hh-demo` with its reason. No em or en dashes; no dates."

Integrator: 12.1 first with 12.4 extending it on the same exported stack (nothing is decommissioned until both have run); 12.2 and 12.3 together. Expected conflict: 12.1 and 12.4 both define how the exported stack is brought up; 12.4's implementation-driven boot is the only one, and 12.1's hand-written configuration is deleted rather than kept as a second path.
Verifier: the export proof runbook records a green readiness report and green suites on the exported stack; the exported stack boots from `impl-hh-demo`'s manifest and the demo seed, and the three walkthroughs run green against it as the one-job-per-micro-app evidence; the secret and hostname scans green over the product bundle and the implementation alike; the old ports answer nothing; the archive READMEs exist; the handover names every package.
Human checkpoints: start (Colton confirms the retention window has passed and the export target machine); mid (none); end (Colton signs off the export proof; the droplet operator confirms decommission).
Exit test: the exported bundle boots from `impl-hh-demo` and the demo seed and passes the readiness report and both suites outside the platform, the three walkthroughs run green against it and stand as the one-job-per-micro-app evidence, the old deployments answer nothing, both old repositories are archived with a pointer, `hh-demo` is recorded as retained rather than decommissioned, and the Gable handover names every shared package with its tests.
Produces: the export proof, including its run over `impl-hh-demo` with the walkthroughs as evidence; the decommission record with `hh-demo` retained; the Gable handover.
Unlocks: G-1 (Gable on the shape with the shared packages present, the tour convention already proven, and an implementation repository worth copying for `impl-gable-demo`).
