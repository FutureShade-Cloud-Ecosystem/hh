Buying and Stock: the testers' brief

One page about what this slice is, what it does, and what it does not do yet. Everything here is plain speech: if the box and this page disagree, the box is wrong and that is a bug worth reporting.

What this is

This is the procurement manager's slice, signed in as Procurement Manager, on the Buying and Stock board, and the board now carries three areas on one bar: Purchasing (this desk: the open purchase orders, the incoming list, the reorder worklist, and drafting, placing and tracking a purchase order), Inventory (the stock truth: lookups, browse, branch stock, reservations, transfers, receiving and the replenishment list) and Cycle counts (count sheets and the inventory to GL reconciliation). Three roles hold cards on the front door: Procurement manager, Yard manager and Payables and Receivables. Vendor bills stay later (Payables and Receivables posts them in the Money Desk).

How to sign in

On the front door, tap Procurement manager and the Buying and Stock board opens under that role's name. The card's line is "Buy the branch's stock and track what is incoming." The tile the card opens is Buying and Stock; the desk inside the tile is the procurement manager's, with the tabs Purchase orders, Incoming, Vendors (later) and Vendor bills (later), and the area bar above carries Purchasing, Inventory and Cycle counts. On this lane the role is live: the card is tappable, the sign-in rides the front door's handoff, and no second sign-in is asked for.

The Buying and Stock runs one role on this lane, the Procurement manager (this one). The other roles have their own cards on the same front door; a card a tester can tap opens its own app, and a card marked Coming cannot be tapped yet.

The demo world

The desk carries the same invented dealer as the rest of this box (Gable Landscape Supply), with two branches, Point Loma and Julian. Every product code begins with DEMO- and every vendor name is invented. The procurement world is the seeded product catalogue and the procurement lane's own seeded suppliers and orders.

  • Three demo suppliers: VND-01 Granite Run Quarry, thirty-day terms, an ordering desk email on file; VND-02 Copperline Pavers, thirty-day terms, an ordering desk email on file; VND-03 Heronfield Stone, fifteen-day terms, no ordering desk email on file yet. The order book's other vendors (the warranty set and the yard catalogue's own) are not the demo vendor set; the tester's edits stay on the demo set.
  • Two open purchase orders: DEMO-PLM-P01 at Point Loma, vendor VND-02, ORDERED, expected in three days, two lines (P02 six hundred and P03 two hundred forty, the P02 and P03 codes the seed plants); DEMO-JUL-P02 at Julian, vendor VND-01, ORDERED, expected in five days, two lines (P08 forty and P10 one hundred).
  • Ten products the seed carries by code: P01 DEMO-PAV-CHARCOAL Interlocking paver 60 mm, charcoal, P02 DEMO-PAV-SANDSTONE Dimensional paver, sandstone, P03 DEMO-PAV-BASALT Large format paver, basalt, P04 DEMO-WALL-BLOCK Retaining wall block 8x18, P05 DEMO-STEP-SLAB Step Slab 24x48 Natural, P06 DEMO-AGG-BEDDING Paver bedding stone, P07 DEMO-AGG-BASE Class 2 base rock, P08 DEMO-AGG-SAND Concrete Sand, P09 DEMO-MULCH-BLACK Black cedar mulch, P10 DEMO-EDGE-ALU Aluminum edging 8 ft.

The procurement gap, by name, is K08: the pick K08 left the pile of P07 Class 2 base rock at Point Loma down to fines, owing a customer stock the pile cannot cover, and there is no inbound purchase order covering it. The seed plants this gap on purpose: P07 at Point Loma sits below its reorder point and no seeded order buys it, so nothing arriving can close it. The buyer's whole story is turning that gap into a placed order, and the worklist is the place it starts. The gap is the worklist's highlighted, picked-on-landing row; the screen carries no note besides the row itself and the Draft PO button, so the story a tester follows is the row and the press.

The seed carries two other gaps alongside K08, on the same demo image: P05 (Step Slab 24x48) sits below its reorder point at Point Loma, and P09 (Black cedar mulch) sits below its reorder point at Julian. This desk reads only its own branch, so the buyer signs in to the two Point Loma gaps, K08 hot on top and the Step Slab gap beside it; the mulch gap sits at Julian, outside this buyer's reach, and never reaches this desk. The seeded orders buy only four products (P02, P03, P08 and P10) that never appear on the reorder worklist, so no seeded order can close a gap the buyer is meant to find.

The home, the tiles and the worklist

The home opens with four tiles: Open purchase orders (the count of orders still open: ordered or partial), Drafts waiting (the count of drafts the buyer has not yet placed), Lines below reorder point (the worklist's count, the figure that is hot when it is non-zero, the empty state when it is zero), Incoming (the count of placed orders with a promised date). On this demo image the figures are: open one, drafts zero, lines below two (K08 and the Step Slab gap), incoming one. Every count reads only this desk's own branch: the seeded Julian order and the Julian gap are another buyer's read.

The reorder worklist sits below the tiles. Every managed line whose projected position fell below its reorder point, worst first, inside the buyer's branch reach. The columns are SKU, product, on hand, reserved, in transit, on order, projected, point, short by, suggest, vendor. The K08 row is the highlighted, picked-on-landing row: P07 Class 2 base rock at Point Loma, the gap the seed plants on purpose, the row the buyer's draft covers first. The other row is P05 Step Slab at Point Loma, the seed's other Point Loma gap, in the same columns; the buyer's first draft covers K08 only. The seed's third gap (P09 Black cedar mulch) sits at Julian, outside this buyer's reach, and never shows on this worklist.

On a fresh box the worklist is the seeded two, K08 hot on top. The order the buyer drafts covers K08 and only K08; the Step Slab gap stays on the worklist until its own order lands.

Drafting a purchase order

The draft opens with the shortfall row already in it: one line, the product's code, the suggested quantity (the server's answer, not the app's), the unit cost (the branch weighted average the seed plants). The form carries the vendor, the expected arrival, the ship to, the delivery method (Vendor truck or Pickup; nothing is lit until one is chosen, and pressing the lit one again clears it). Save draft, Place order, Cancel draft sit at the bottom, and the line actions (Change, Remove, Add a line) sit on the lines themselves.

A draft's lines are editable while it is a draft. Change opens the line's editor for the quantity and the unit cost, the two figures a vendor's confirmation corrects; the product is not editable (that is a different line: remove this one and add it). Add a line lists the active catalogue, and Remove takes a line off. Once the order is placed the lines freeze: the server refuses the edit in its own words.

Expected arrival takes a date. The field is the browser's own date control, and under it the words read the date out as it is typed (Tuesday 4 March 2031 (in 5 days)). The form checks the date before it is sent, a guard with three plain refusals for an empty, a wrongly shaped and a not real date; the browser's date field makes the guard hard to trip from the screen. The server holds the same line and refuses a bad shape in its own words.

An empty draft is refused in the server's own words, beside the button. Remove the line and the press answers Cannot order an empty purchase order, add at least one line.; the draft sits open until a line is back or the draft is cancelled.

Place order sits the line on the register and under Incoming. The notice names the new PO number, given by the server and never typed by anyone, the register row reads Ordered, the worklist row drops the picked mark, and the tiles move. The draft form is gone, the home is back.

Cancel draft cancels the order it drafted. Press it and the draft panel closes, the worklist row is plain again and the tiles move back. The cancelled order stays in the register, marked Cancelled, and the placement never happened. A draft waiting shows in the register too, marked Draft, until it is placed or cancelled.

The register and the incoming view

The register on the right reads the purchase orders with their states, Ordered, Partial and Received as outline words. A state is never a button: receiving moves an order from Ordered through Partial to Received as its stock lands, and nothing on this desk sets those states by hand. The order is the server's, newest placed first; the value is the order's money, the status is the order's state. The seeded Point Loma PO sits at the top of the register; the seeded Julian PO is another buyer's read.

Incoming is the list of placed orders with something still to arrive. The rows list the PO, the vendor and the expected date (as in 3 days or no date, never an invented calendar). On a wide screen each row also says how it is coming (Vendor truck, Pickup, or not set yet) and its line count; on a phone those two columns are hidden. A missing date reads no date, never a placeholder. The seeded Point Loma PO sits on the incoming list with its seeded expected date, in three days.

Each desk reads only its own branch. A Julian PO never shows on the Point Loma desk; the worklist, the register and the incoming list all read only the buyer's own branch. The Point Loma buyer reads the Point Loma rows, the seeded DEMO-PLM-P01; the Julian rows, the seeded DEMO-JUL-P02, are a different buyer's read. A read that names a branch outside the buyer's reach is refused in the server's own words, You do not have access to that branch.

Receiving, vendors, vendor bills

Receiving is no longer only the yard's. The Purchasing tab bar still names Receiving as later on that desk, and the receiving itself now lives one area over: the Inventory area's Receive tab takes a placed order, asks first with the order, the yard and the quantities named, and lands the stock when you confirm. The procurement desk stays the screen that drafts and tracks the order; the Inventory area is the screen that closes the gap.

Vendors is later. The tab on the bar is dashed between the others, and the screen names it in plain text. The vendor list, the create, the edit and the deactivate are later items; the desk has the desk's contact, the SKUs and the lead for each vendor, and the rest is later. When the create comes, a vendor made here gets its number from the server, minted under the VM- prefix, never typed, and a seeded supplier keeps its own number.

Vendor bills is later. There is no vendor bill control on this desk; the screen names the words Posting a vendor bill is for Payables and Receivables. The Procurement Manager can read the bills against its own orders and the three way match, but does not post them. The three way match is a Money Desk screen, not a Buying and Stock one.

The Inventory area

The area bar under the header switches between Purchasing, Inventory and Cycle counts, and the card still lands on Purchasing. The Inventory area carries eight tabs: Stock lookup, Browse, Branch stock, Pending fulfilment, Reservations, Transfers, Receive and Replenishment.

Stock lookup takes a product from a picker (by SKU and name, never a bare id) and answers the per-branch buckets (on hand, reserved, available) with the weighted-average cost, and the move ledger under it. A product that has never moved says so plainly: No stock has moved for this product yet. That is not an error, and a failed lookup never says it for one.

Browse searches the catalogue and answers each row with its stock position beside it, the total before the page (Showing 4 of 4 products), and the facet counts.

Branch stock reads one product at every yard, including the yards that hold none. A negative available is stock sold before its receipt, by design, and it is named where it stands.

Reservations lists the active holds and takes a new one (product, branch, quantity, for an order or a quote). Only a hard hold subtracts from available, and the branch allocation policy is on the same screen. Release takes a hold back; the expiry sweep runs on a press.

Transfers shows the trucks as the three events they are: a draft raised, a truck loaded (ship), a truck unloaded (receive). Ship pre-fills the expected arrival from the route's configured transit day, and the words under the box say what the default is. A shipped transfer offers no cancel: the correction is a transfer back. Shipping more than the yard holds is refused in the server's own words, and a same-branch raise is refused in words. The transit-time table reads only: setting a route's time is the tech admin's.

Receive lists the buyer's placed orders with what is still outstanding. Pressing Receive asks first, naming the order, the yard and every line's quantity, and nothing moves until you confirm. The receipt's notice names the lines and the value that hit the goods-not-invoiced account, and the order leaves the list.

Replenishment lists the managed lines and their reorder points. Change opens an editor that names the product, the branch and the figures it starts from; the nudge posts one message to procurement per product and branch within the posting cycle (a second press says so); the bootstrap opens a line for every stocked product at half of today's on hand, and never overwrites a line a person tuned.

The Cycle counts area

Count sheets lists a branch's sheets with their scope and state (Open, Frozen, Counted, Posted, Cancelled). New sheet takes the branch and the scope (a full count or an ad-hoc one), and opens the sheet.

The count entry freezes the snapshot first: until the freeze there is nothing to count against. Counting is blind by default (the book on-hand is hidden until you lift it), every counted figure is typed, and every variance needs a reason code before the sheet submits. The counter is the maker.

The variance pane sums the sheet: variance lines, net, gross. A checker who is not the maker approves, and the post asks first, naming the sheet, the yard, the line count and the net value. The post writes the stock moves and the reason-coded journal, and posting again never doubles it. The maker approving their own count is refused in the server's words; so is a post with no checker's approval.

Reconciliation runs the inventory to GL tie-out once per period and branch on the first look (the subledger against the GL's inventory account), keeps its history, and lists the by-design negative stock a count should clear.

The walls

Drafting, placing, editing and cancelling a purchase order is for the Procurement Manager, the General Manager and the super admin only. Press the Draft PO button under another sign-in and the words say so, the shared refusal the role's walls answer with, in plain words: Drafting, placing, editing and cancelling a purchase order is for the Procurement Manager, the General Manager and the super admin only. The Yard Manager receives goods only; other roles can read. The bare word Forbidden is not on screen.

The Yard Manager receives goods and counts. The yard manager's sign-in opens the same board: receiving a placed order and working a count sheet are the yard manager's moves, and the manager seats' moves (holding stock, moving it between branches, approving and posting a count) are refused in words that name who may. A read-only sign-in (Payables and Receivables) can look at everything and is refused every write in the same way.

Known issues you will meet

  • The world is rebuilt to the moment the box was rebuilt, so what you see always reads as current. The next rebuild may carry a fresh seed, and the figures you saw last time may not match the figures you see this time. The shape stays the same.
  • A restart of the box signs everyone out. Sign in again from the role cards.
  • Signing out ends the session. Reloading the page in the same tab keeps the session until you sign out, and a new tab starts at the front door.
  • On a phone the draft fits. The line, its amount and the total are on screen; the page does not scroll sideways. Every worklist figure is on screen, none hidden behind a sideways scroll.
  • With browser developer tools, a technical tester reaches a few reads beyond the desk, and most of them are walled. The Yard Manager's reads are refused for this role, the Yard Manager's sign-in is refused for this role, and the desk refuses its own card under another role in the same words. The wall work for the rest of the routes is a later item, and no General Manager login exists on this box.
