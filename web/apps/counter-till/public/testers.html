Counter Till: the testers' brief

One page about what this box is, what it does, and what it does not do yet. Everything here is plain speech: if the box and this page disagree, the box is wrong and that is a bug worth reporting.

What this is

This is the new HH: a new foundation, built one module at a time, where your feedback now shapes what gets built. The first module is the Counter Till: the counter checkout, the desk where a customer's order is rung, tendered and receipted.

You already hold the older app and its role demo with the walkthroughs. That older demo stays open, and it remains the fuller picture of what exists: this box is one module of the new foundation, not a replacement for it. The Counter Till has a direct counterpart in the older demo's counter checkout flow, so you can compare the two side by side.

How to sign in

Signing in is a tap: tap Sales or Sales manager and the till opens under that role's name.

Two roles work at this till in this release. Five roles work on the box: the two here, and Payables and Receivables, the Procurement manager and Driver, who each sign in at the front door because the till serves the counter. The other nine are listed as Coming and cannot be tapped: Outside Sales, Yard Team, Yard Manager, Logistics Manager, Branch Manager, Payables and Receivables, Financial Controller, General Manager, and HR.

What the two working roles do differently is the manager's wider reach, which has no screen of its own in this release.

The demo world

The box carries one invented dealer, Hardscape House (demo), with two branches, Trenton and Kingston, and the counter works Trenton. Every product and account on it is invented for the demo.

There are ten demo products and nine demo customers, and every one of their names carries (demo). Every product code begins with DEMO-. Before each round of testing the box is wiped back to its starting world, and the world's history is written again at that moment: the box is dated to the moment it was rebuilt, so what you see always reads as current.

Two kinds of sale

The till rings a counter sale: every line is priced by the pricing engine at that customer's own price, and there is no price field to type into. What a customer pays comes from the dealer's pricing rules and tiers, never from the person at the counter. The tax and the total are the ring's own answer too: until a sale is rung, the till shows the lines' prices and their subtotal, and the total appears when the sale is rung.

A margin column can sit beside each price, exactly as the dealer's margin visibility policy answers: on this box the policy lets the manager and above see margin and estimated profit, so the Sales manager's till shows the column and the Sales clerk's till shows none. Since the hardening train the glance follows the same word as the till, so the clerk's own margin read answers nothing at all; the manager's read is the proof the figures exist, and the clerk's screen states no margin anywhere: no column, no margin or profit figures in the footer bar, and no eye to hide them with.

The till asks no floor. A contractor's price can sit below the dealer's floor margin, and at the counter that line rings anyway; the manager's till shows the low margin in its column, and the clerk's, refused by the same policy word, rings the line without one. That is on purpose: the floor belongs to the quote flow, and it arrives with Quote Desk, the module after this one.

The other kind of sale is the quote, and it is not on this box yet: a quote is agreed at its price and becomes an order through its own flow, and that flow is where the floor margin lives. It comes with Quote Desk.

Tenders and receipts

Cash asks how much was tendered; the change due is the ring's own answer, stated on the confirmation and shown on the receipt. Before the sale is rung the till states no change figure, even with an amount typed.

Cards are simulated. A card sale says Simulated card: no card was charged. No card number is ever typed anywhere: there is no field for one.

An account sale bills the customer's account: the receipt names the invoice, and the account's card in the Customer step shows the fresh balance.

Refunds

The returns desk starts with the customer, like a new order. Find the customer, and every order they hold is listed with its lines, grouped by order and job. Pick lines from several orders into one credit, choose for each line whether it goes back to stock, is damaged, or comes back as nothing returned, and watch the credit so far. Then choose how it goes back (card where the sale was paid by card, on the account, or cash from the drawer) and record it: one credit note, each order's return named with its number, and for cash the drawer's expected cash drops by the note's total. A credit note over the dealer's approval amount, where a dealer sets one, asks for a manager's card; this demo dealer sets none.

The walls

A sale rung with no customer can be returned too: on the first step choose Walk in sale and type its receipt or order number. Walk in sales are never listed, only found by their number, and they go back to cash or card, never to an account.

The swap: on the Refund step, add the replacement goods and the credit pays for them. The screen shows the credit, the new order total with tax, what the credit pays, and either what goes back by the original way the sale was paid or what is still to pay. Ring the swap and one credit note and one new order are recorded, each naming the other, with a receipt line for each.

One branch never sees another's. A sale that belongs to Kingston is refused at this counter before any money moves, whichever of the three refund ways is tried, even though the sale is real; the cash and account rails name the branch fence, and the card rail refuses a seeded card sale first for its missing card reference. A credit note covers one branch only.

Each role reaches only its own screens. Beyond the till, the matrix holds on this box too: through browser tools, the manager's sign-in reaches dispatch reads that the clerk's is refused, though no screen for them exists in this release.

The credit gate refuses an account sale in words, to both logins: an account over its limit, or on a hold, cannot carry a sale on account. On the zero limit walk-up account you will meet the shape refusal instead, the same words to both logins: this account cannot carry a sale on account at all, so the till answers in the server's own words, This account cannot carry $6.72 on account. Pay in full now, or take a deposit with the balance due later., and nothing is rung; the ticket stands. Credit exceptions belong to leadership, and no leadership login exists in this release, so nobody on this box can release a hold.

A ticket can be parked and picked up again by anyone at the same counter, whatever role they signed in as. The parked list is this branch's own: a ticket parked at Kingston never shows at Trenton.

The drawer

One shared drawer serves each branch's counter, and every tester rings into the same drawer, so its count will not balance. That is expected: closing and counting the drawer is not in this release. On a fresh box the drawer starts closed: Sales presses Open Session, and the drawer opens with a 300.00 float, the reference till's own.

Known issues you will meet

  • The card sales that come with the starting world carry no card reference, so a card refund of one is refused. Refund such a sale in cash or to the account, or return a card sale you rang yourself in this round.
  • A restart of the box signs everyone out. Sign in again from the role cards.
  • Signing out ends the session. Reloading the page in the same tab keeps the session until you sign out, and a new tab starts at the front door. A reload still drops the ticket being built.
  • With browser developer tools, a technical tester reaches a few reads beyond the till, and none of them is fenced to a branch yet: the manager's sign-in reaches the will-call queue and the scan trail, either sign-in reaches the transaction trail, and the audit list stays walled to both. This is known, and it is named here rather than hidden: the fence work for those reads is a later item, and no Branch Manager login exists on this box.

Where feedback goes

One channel: send everything to Colton, and he files every note with the principal in one place. What helps most: what you tapped, what you expected, and what you saw instead. The older demo's feedback path stays open too, but what you say about this box is what decides what gets built next.
