Counter Till: the testers' brief

One page about what this box is, what it does, and what it does not do yet. Everything here is plain speech: if the box and this page disagree, the box is wrong and that is a bug worth reporting.

What this is

This is the new HH: a new foundation, built one module at a time, where your feedback now shapes what gets built. The first module is the Counter Till: the counter checkout, the desk where a customer's order is rung, tendered and receipted.

You already hold the older app and its role demo with the walkthroughs. That older demo stays open, and it remains the fuller picture of what exists: this box is one module of the new foundation, not a replacement for it. The Counter Till has a direct counterpart in the older demo's counter checkout flow, so you can compare the two side by side.

How to sign in

Signing in is a tap: tap Sales or Sales manager and the till opens under that role's name.

Two roles work in this release. The other eleven are listed as Coming and cannot be tapped: Outside Sales, Yard Team, Yard Manager, Logistics Manager, Driver, Branch Manager, Procurement Manager, Payables and Receivables, Financial Controller, General Manager, and HR.

What the two working roles do differently is the big refund (below), and the manager's wider reach, which has no screen of its own in this release.

The demo world

The box carries one invented dealer, Hardscape House (demo), with two branches, Trenton and Kingston, and the counter works Trenton. Every product and account on it is invented for the demo.

There are ten demo products and nine demo customers, and every one of their names carries (demo). Every product code begins with DEMO-. Beside the nine, the account picker also lists a few placeholder rows left over from the starting data, named as scrubbed placeholders: part of the invented world, not accounts to bill.

Before each round of testing the box is wiped back to its starting world, and the world's history is written again at that moment: the box is dated to the moment it was rebuilt, so what you see always reads as current.

Two kinds of sale

The till rings a counter sale: every line is priced by the pricing engine at that customer's own price, and there is no price field to type into. What a customer pays comes from the dealer's pricing rules and tiers, never from the person at the counter.

Beside each price sits a margin column, and it answers for both logins: who sees cost and margin is the dealer's own configuration.

The till asks no floor. A contractor's price can sit below the dealer's floor margin, and at the counter that line rings anyway, with the low margin shown in the column. That is on purpose: the floor belongs to the quote flow, and it arrives with Quote Desk, the module after this one.

The other kind of sale is the quote, and it is not on this box yet: a quote is agreed at its price and becomes an order through its own flow, and that flow is where the floor margin lives. It comes with Quote Desk.

Tenders and receipts

Cash asks how much was tendered and shows the change due on the receipt.

Cards are simulated. A card sale says Simulated card: no card was charged, and a card refund says that nothing reached a card. No card number is ever typed anywhere: there is no field for one.

An account sale bills the customer's account: the receipt names the invoice, and the account's card in the Customer step shows the fresh balance.

Refunds, and the manager walking over

The returns desk finds a sale by its number. A refund within the dealer's approval amount, 250.00, commits for the clerk, and a cash refund comes out of the drawer: the drawer's expected cash drops by it.

A refund above 250.00 is refused to the clerk, and the refusal names the amount and the roles that may approve it: the Sales manager or the Branch manager.

That is when the manager walks over. At the clerk's till, the manager taps their own card, the same refund goes through once, and the receipt records it as the manager's decision with the clerk named beside. The till stays the clerk's: signing in as the manager was one tap on one dialog, not a change of seats.

Signed in as the Sales manager, the same refund completes at once with no second approval.

The walls

One branch never sees another's. A sale that belongs to Kingston is refused at this counter before any money moves, whichever of the three refund ways is tried, even though the sale number is real; the cash and account rails name the branch fence, and the card rail refuses a seeded card sale first for its missing card reference.

Each role reaches only its own screens. Beyond the till, the matrix holds on this box too: through browser tools, the manager's sign-in reaches dispatch reads that the clerk's is refused, though no screen for them exists in this release.

The credit gate refuses an account sale in words, to both logins: an account over its limit, or on a hold, cannot carry a sale on account. At this counter you will meet that refusal on the zero limit walk-up account; cash and card still work. Credit exceptions belong to leadership, and no leadership login exists in this release, so nobody on this box can release a hold.

A ticket can be parked and picked up again by anyone at the same counter, whatever role they signed in as. The parked list is this branch's own: a ticket parked at Kingston never shows at Trenton.

The drawer

One shared drawer serves each branch's counter, and every tester rings into the same drawer, so its count will not balance. That is expected: closing and counting the drawer is not in this release. On a fresh box the drawer starts closed: Sales presses Open Session, and the drawer opens with a 300.00 float, the reference till's own.

Known issues you will meet

  • The card sales that come with the starting world carry no card reference, so a card refund of one is refused. Refund such a sale in cash or to the account, or return a card sale you rang yourself in this round.
  • A restart of the box signs everyone out. Sign in again from the role cards.
  • Signing out, or just reloading the page, ends the session: the till keeps no session between visits. A reload also drops the ticket being built: nothing is kept between visits.
  • With browser developer tools, a technical tester reaches a few reads beyond the till, and none of them is fenced to a branch yet: the manager's sign-in reaches the will-call queue and the scan trail, either sign-in reaches the transaction trail, and the audit list stays walled to both. This is known, and it is named here rather than hidden: the fence work for those reads is a later item, and no Branch Manager login exists on this box.

Where feedback goes

One channel: send everything to Colton, and he files every note with the principal in one place. What helps most: what you tapped, what you expected, and what you saw instead. The older demo's feedback path stays open too, but what you say about this box is what decides what gets built next.
