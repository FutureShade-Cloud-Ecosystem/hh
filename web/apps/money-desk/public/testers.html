Demo box: everything on it is invented. No money is owed, paid or moved by this app, and no decision made here reaches a real book, a real branch or a real approval.

Money Desk: the testers' brief

One page about what the Money Desk does and what it does not do yet. If the box and this page disagree, the box is wrong and that is a bug worth reporting.

What this is

The Money Desk has two sides. The receivables side is for the Payables and Receivables role: it reads the book's aging, works the bounced payment the queue shows, takes a payment in against open invoices, records a promise to pay and runs a statement. The ledger side is for the Financial Controller: it decides the approvals queue, approves a parked journal, reads the journal register and the trial balance. The controller holds both sides.

How to sign in

On the front door, tap Payables and Receivables ("Chase overdue accounts and work the bounced payments.") or Financial Controller ("Decide approvals and keep the ledger."). Each card shows a Money Desk tile, and the tile opens the same Money Desk with no second sign in.

Payables and Receivables lands on the receivables side.

The Financial Controller lands on the ledger side, Approvals and ledger, with a Receivables switch beside it. One tap opens the receivables side whole.

The Sales and Sales manager cards offer no Money Desk tile. The other nine roles on the front door are listed as Coming and cannot be tapped.

If you open the Money Desk without the front door, its own sign in offers only the two roles that can use it.

No General Manager sign in exists on this box. Anything the screens say is the General Manager's to decide has no one to decide it here.

The receivables world

The receivables side works the book's 18 invoices, spread across the aging buckets, and one bounced payment.

The bounced payment is the cheque for the whole of one Trenton contractor invoice, returned by the bank with the reason "Cheque returned by the bank, not sufficient funds" and a policy fee of $45.00. The payment is DEMO-PAY-B01 and the cheque reference is DEMO-CHQ-B01, which the queue's reference search finds. No allocation remains on it, so that invoice stands open for its full amount.

The seeded book plants no credit hold. A hold appears when the sweep places one, or when a payment you mark bounced meets the dealer's returned payment policy.

Receivables: the tiles and the register

The receivables home opens with four aging tiles in this order: Escalation, Late, Overdue, Current. Each figure is the server's. A tile filters the register to the accounts it counted, and tapping it again clears the filter.

The register lists open invoices, overdue first. A row's status is a word with the day count, not a colour alone, and a promise or a dispute rides the row as a second mark. The register has filters for overdue, promised, disputed and paid, and Download CSV hands over the register.

Needs you sits beside the register and holds the bounced payment, the accounts on credit hold and the paper path item. Remind on an account, and the paper path item, chase what is overdue.

The Payables tab is marked later. There is no Payables control on this release.

Receivables: taking a payment

Record a payment on the home asks which account first. On an invoice row it opens that account with the payment ready.

One payment can cover several invoices. Pick the invoices, type the amount and the screen shows the server's split, oldest first, with the words "closes" or "part" against each invoice and what stays open. The screen computes no figure of its own: it sends the amount, the method, the reference and the picked invoices, and draws the server's answer. Recording posts one payment, and the balances that show afterwards are the server's.

The desk records a payment that has already come in. It charges no card and asks for no card number: the method is a choice and the reference is a cheque number or transfer reference.

If the answer to Record payment is lost, or the server answers with a failure of its own, the screen says the payment may or may not have been recorded and tells you to check the account's payments and balances before taking the money again. On a phone the message is brought into view and focused when it appears. Press Record payment again to send the same payment: the desk holds the payment's key and sends it again, so the payment cannot post twice. If the earlier press was recorded and the form has since become a different payment, the server refuses it in words: the screen says this payment was not recorded and the earlier press was recorded as a different payment, shows those words as an error (never a success notice), keeps the form filled, and the next press records the cheque as a new payment with a fresh key. A payment on another account never disturbs a held key. The desk keeps a held key until the payment route itself answers it, so looking at an invoice's payments, a sign out and sign in as the same person, or a reload of the page does not end it, and another person signing in never sees it. If your sign in ends while a key is held, the screen says the earlier press may or may not have been recorded and tells you to sign in and then press Record payment; if your role is refused, it names who may record the payment and says to press again only once you are signed in with a role that can. The same key goes out.

When the server refuses a payment in words, the screen says the payment was not recorded and shows the server's own sentence, for example that the selected invoices can take at most a stated amount. A pick of more than 100 invoices never reaches the server: the screen refuses it itself, with the route's own words for that ceiling, and a held key is untouched. A refusal writes nothing, and the next press is a new payment. The replay refusals are the one exception: when the server says the key already recorded a different payment, the screen says this payment was not recorded, keeps the form, and the next press records it as a new payment; when it says the key recorded a payment for another account, the screen reads it as the earlier payment being recorded and refreshes the account.

A paid invoice stays on the account for context, dimmed, and takes nothing.

A promise to pay is recorded against an invoice. A second promise on the same invoice is refused in the server's own words.

Receivables: bounced payments, statements and holds

A payment can be marked bounced from its invoice, with a reason, and it joins the queue. In the queue it is worked three ways: a replacement payment, a promise to pay, or a reminder to the account.

An account's Statement tab has none until you run it. Run a statement to read the aging strip, then send it to the AP contact. Download PDF, the Activity tab and the Contacts tab are marked later.

The Statements tab lists every account, and Run for every account runs them all.

The Credit holds tab lists the holds. For Payables and Receivables, Lift and Override are held. Pressing Lift says in words that releasing a hold is for the Financial Controller or the General Manager, and the bare word Forbidden is not shown. Run the sweep is refused in words as the Financial Controller's. Place a hold is marked later: the core has no route for it yet.

The Financial Controller lifts a hold with a reason, or overrides another, and has no Place a hold button.

Approving a parked journal and releasing a credit hold are refused to Payables and Receivables by the server, in its shared refusal body. Payables and Receivables has no control for either on its screens.

The ledger: the approvals queue

The controller's queue holds a below floor quote request (twelve percent asked on one quote line), two parked journals and the freight raises of both branches. One parked journal is a $7,500.00 prepaid insurance premium made by the Sales manager login, the other is a $6,200.00 inventory write-down made by the controller itself. Both sit over the $5,000.00 approval threshold. There is one freight raise in each branch.

The queue is told apart by kind: Below floor quote request, Journal parked over the threshold, Freight above the quote. Each journal is named by its number, and the controller's own request says Filed by you.

Choosing the floor request opens its decision. The panel reads the core's sentence in words, with the quote, line, discount asked, margin asked and floor margin as tiles, and says "One decision, one quote." The approval is spent on that one quote. The dollar tiles for the asked price, the floor and the list are not drawn yet and say later, and approving at a different price is marked later.

Choosing a parked journal shows its amount and its lines. Deciding to approve posts the journal in the same step: the register then shows it as Posted and the trial balance carries it.

The controller cannot decide its own request. The panel says so before a press, both choices are held with the words "Held: not yours to decide.", and Decide is disabled. The way out it names is the General Manager.

A refused decision names who may decide it, the Financial Controller or the General Manager, and tells you to sign out and in again if this was not the role you meant. The bare word Forbidden is not shown, and the request stays in the queue.

A queue answer that is not the page the contract describes is refused, never half read: the screen says the approvals list was not readable and draws no rows.

On a phone, choosing a request brings its decision into view.

The ledger: journals and the trial balance

The Journals tab lists the journals with filters that carry counts, such as Pending. A journal number never wraps onto two lines. Void and Reverse are marked later.

The Trial balance tab reads in balance with the server's totals, and takes All branches or one branch. The tie out, choosing a period and the Excel download are marked later.

Bank reconciliation

The Financial Controller has a third area, Bank reconciliation, beside Approvals and ledger and Receivables. Payables and Receivables does not have it. It lists the operating account and the two cards with the number of lines waiting on each, and the cards are marked tracking only.

Import takes a statement as a chosen file or as pasted rows. Importing the same rows again adds nothing and says so. A file the core cannot read is refused in words that say what it lacked, such as a missing Amount column, a date it does not read or an amount that is not money. Uploading to storage and a bank feed are marked later.

Auto match pairs the card settlements, the cash and cheque lodgements and the batch settlements to what the till received, and leaves the rest. It can be held to a window of dates. Debits such as fees are matched by hand.

A line is matched by hand to a payment, a lodgement, a posted journal or a group. A payment or lodgement posts its clearing entry; a journal posts nothing new. A refused match says what to do, and an id that is not an id is refused before anything is sent.

Undo match asks first, returns the line to unmatched, and the line keeps its history, with the undone match marked as undone.

Opening a month takes the closing balance on the statement and shows the difference in words. The ledger side is the core's. The difference is worked out when the month is opened or refreshed, so refresh the figures after matching. Sign off is held until the difference is zero, and the core refuses it in words while any line is unmatched. A signed off month is closed and cannot be opened again.

A closing balance of zero is not sent quietly: the core replaces a zero with the net of the month's lines, and the screen says so. A blank or invalid balance is refused in the browser and nothing is sent.

The Rules tab lists every version of each match rule and which is in use. Approving a new version puts it in use and retires the one before it.

A refused import, match, undo, month or rule names who may do it, the Financial Controller or a manager, and never shows the bare word Forbidden.

While the accounts load the screen says so, a failed load offers Try again, and an empty list says so. A list that is not the shape the core sends is refused, never half read. More than a page of lines shows more on request.

On a phone each table becomes cards, a chosen line brings its panel into view, and nothing scrolls sideways. Every control works from the keyboard.

Everywhere

On a phone no screen scrolls the page sideways.

An empty world says so in words on each screen: "No invoices in this view.", "Nothing needs you right now." and "No account is on hold."
